A Twitch chat overlay game

Chat plays.
Nobody types a command.

Chatters join a guild by their name colour: red, green or blue. Just by chatting, they grow their guild's land on a hex map and build its Monument. When the streamer wants some action, they start a battle and the chatters' fighters clash on screen, fully automatic.

The streamer takes part too: as the god of the map.

How it works in 30 seconds

  1. 1

    Chat normally

    Chat time is split into short windows of a few minutes. Every chatter who writes at least once in a window earns one tick. One message or fifty: still one tick.

  2. 2

    Colour picks your guild

    Your Twitch name colour puts you in Red, Green or Blue. You stay there for the whole season.

  3. 3

    Land grows, Monuments rise

    Ticks develop your guild's tiles. Developed land feeds the guild's Monument. First Monument to finish wins the season.

  4. 4

    The streamer starts the fights

    Wars, Clashes and god fights run by themselves on the overlay. Chatters are drawn as their latest emote and fight automatically.

Built-in spam protection

More spam is not more help. A chatter earns at most one tick per time window, whether they write one message or flood the chat with fifty. Copy-paste walls, emote spam and one-letter messages add nothing for the guild. What counts is how many people chat, and how often they come back, not how many messages they send. Known bots like Nightbot or StreamElements earn nothing at all.

So the best way to help your guild is the nicest one for the stream too: just be there and talk.

Progress is saved between streams. A season usually lasts many streams, so it matters over weeks, not just one break.

Guilds & the map

There are three guilds. The hue of your Twitch name colour decides which one you join:

  • Red: magenta, red, orange and yellow-orange names
  • Green: yellow, green and teal names
  • Blue: cyan, blue and purple names

No colour set, or a grey one? The game picks a guild from your user id, so it is always the same. In a very small chat, newcomers fill the smallest guild first, so no guild sits empty. Your guild is locked for the season. Changing your colour later does not move you.

The map

The map is a hexagon of 19 tiles. Each guild has a city on a corner. Cities can never be taken, so no guild is ever out. At the start of a season every guild owns five tiles next to its city, and all three already border each other. The center tile belongs to the god.

  • Tile levels 0 to 10. Every chatter has a home tile in their guild, and each tick builds it. Higher levels make a tile harder to take in a War.
  • Uneven growth. New A tile with many regulars shoots ahead while a quiet one lags, and every season some land is more fertile than other land. You can see it: grass on rich land, cracks and stones on poor land.
  • Mishaps. New Lightning seeks the tallest tiles and knocks them down a level or two. A fire burns a level every five minutes until enough of the guild is chatting to put it out, and it can spread to the neighbours. The overlay tells the guild how many more chatters it takes. Both leave their mark for a while: a scorched, cracked tile or a patch of ash. Now and then a good harvest raises a tile.
  • The center tile. A guild can win it, but only holds it for 30 minutes of stream time. Then the god takes it back.

The Monument race

Each guild builds one Monument: an Obelisk, a Ziggurat or a Temple, dealt at random each season. It is the season's finish line.

In every time window in which a guild is active, its Monument grows by 10, plus 10 for every tile the guild holds, plus 10 for every level of those tiles. Even undeveloped land counts. The overlay pops the gains above the Monument as they come in, and a little "+1" beside it each time a chatter of the guild earns a tick. More land and better land means a faster Monument. That is why Wars over land matter.

The first guild to finish its Monument wins the season on the spot. The overlay shows each Monument going up in five stages.

The streamer only picks how long a season should last, in stream hours (10 by default). The game measures how fast your chat builds during the first hours and sets the finish line to match, then keeps it fixed. A small chat and a huge one both get a season of about the same length.

How a season ends

  • A guild finishes its Monument and wins.
  • The god wins 5 fights against chat in the season, and the god wins it.
  • The streamer ends it from the control panel. The furthest Monument wins.

A recap follows on stream: the winner, the most kills, the highest level, the most Clash wins, the best Pachinko run and the god's score, one after another. New

Then the land is dealt again, Monuments, XP and levels reset, and everyone is assigned a guild again from their current colour. Lifetime stats like kills, battles and seasons won are kept.

Battles

The streamer decides when a fight starts. Every fight lasts about a minute to a minute and a half. Fights are fully automatic: fighters walk to the nearest enemy, hit, dodge and use their class skills. A fighter stuck in a crowd now and then breaks out to hunt down a straggler, so the battle keeps moving. Each fighter is drawn as the emote the chatter used last, on a dark disc with a ring in the guild's colour and the name above it.

War

Counts for the season

Three battles, one per guild as attacker. Each guild goes for the best-developed border tile of its biggest neighbour, and the announcement names the stake: "Red attacks Blue's level 8". Win it and the tile is yours, at half its level. Everyone active in the last 30 minutes takes part, up to 40 per side. Moderators can be allowed to start one with !war.

Hero Clash

Counts for the season

The three most active chatters of each guild today fight in three-way heats, then a final. Equal stats for everyone. The winner's guild gets +50% XP for 30 minutes and the winner wears a crown.

Guild Clash

Countdown fight, counts

A 30-second countdown appears. Everyone who writes during it is in the draw, and those who wrote just before fill any places left. Up to 10 per guild are drawn; a guild with fewer gets the streamer's imps in its colour for the empty places. The line-up is shown with everyone's emote so you find yourself at a glance, then 3 per guild fight at once and the bench steps in. Last guild standing gets the Hero Clash reward.

Free-for-all

Countdown fight, just for fun

Same countdown and draw. 30 chatters are drawn, 10 fight at a time, everyone for themselves. The last one standing wins. It changes nothing in the game.

Endless mode

For intros, outros, breaks

A never-ending free-for-all of the active chatters. The streamer picks how many fight at once (2 to 60). Fallen fighters rejoin after 3 seconds. No XP, no stats, just motion on screen.

Obstacles New

Battles can get obstacles, picked at random for each battle. The streamer can switch them off.

  • Walls that stand still and block the way.
  • Barrels that roll across the arena.
  • Bumpers like on a pinball table, bouncing chatters away.
  • Spike traps that glow red, then strike everyone standing on them.

About two in five team battles are fought in a named arena: the Pinball Hall, the Fortress, the Brewery, the Spike Pit, the Ruins, the Funhouse or the Gauntlet. Each has a layout of its own and changes during a long fight. The god's Rampage smashes through walls, and a Shockwave sends chatters bouncing between bumpers. In a free-for-all and in endless mode, obstacles appear and disappear during the fight.

Fair fights

  • Levels give a small health bonus, capped at +30%, so veterans do not flatten newcomers.
  • Smaller guilds get stronger fighters, so a guild half the size still wins about half the time.
  • A bit of luck each battle keeps upsets possible.
  • Countdown fights use equal stats: nothing is decided before the fight. The 15-second line-up is a good moment for chat bets.
  • During any battle the panels make room for the fight. A slim map banner across the top or bottom of the screen keeps the map and the Monument race in view; nothing in the fight ever covers it. New
  • Highlights beside the scoreboard call out the moments that matter, with who did it: first blood, a streak ended, the crown falling, a barrel strike. Snarky on purpose. New

Classes

Every chatter gets a random class for each fight. A small badge on the fighter shows which one. Classes keep fights moving instead of two lines trading hits.

Fighter 30%

Walks to the nearest enemy. With two or more around, it spins and hits them all.

Archer 20%

Shoots from a distance and backs away from anyone who gets close.

Tank 15%

Slow, with 50% more health. Enemies nearby go for it first. Raises a shield when things get hot.

Thief 15%

Fast. Hunts archers, mages and healers. Strikes, darts back, and slips into the shadows.

Mage 10%

Marks a rune circle on the ground. A moment later it blasts everyone inside.

Healer 10%

Follows the most hurt ally and heals it. Alone, it fights weakly for itself.

The percentages are how often each class is dealt.

The god: the streamer

The streamer does not join a guild. Instead they descend as the god of the map: one big fighter with the streamer's name, colour and latest emote (or channel picture). The streamer only chooses when. The fight itself runs on its own, like every other.

God's Wrath

Streamer vs the leading guild

The god attacks the guild with the most tiles, on its tile farthest from its city. If the god wins, the tile turns neutral and is drawn scorched in the streamer's colour. If the guild wins, it keeps the tile and its fighters get bonus XP. A brake on the leader.

Streamer vs Chat

Streamer vs everyone

A boss fight against all three guilds at once. If chat wins, everyone gets bonus XP. If the god wins the first one of a stream, it lays waste to the map: every guild loses a tile and every Monument loses 15%. Later fights in the same stream are friendly: nothing is at stake.

The god's health grows with the size of chat, so it is a fair fight for 5 chatters or 100. The god wins if it is still standing when time runs out, so chat has to bring it down. The overlay keeps the score, like "God 3 : 2 Chat". Win 5 times in a season and the god wins the season.

The god's skills

Every few seconds the god casts a skill, announced in big letters a second ahead. The streamer can switch each one on or off.

Between skills the god never stands still: it dashes through the crowd, knocking chatters aside, blinks to another spot in a puff of smoke, or runs off one side of the screen and storms back in at the other. Chatters cannot follow it there. New

Arena: just for fun

The Arena tab in the control panel has fights that change nothing in the game. Good for breaks and for betting in chat.

  • Free-for-all with a countdown and a line-up.
  • Friendly Streamer vs Chat, as often as the streamer likes.
  • Endless mode for intros and outros.
  • Test battles between chosen guilds.

Pachinko New

Chatters are picked like in a Guild Clash: write during the countdown to be in the draw. But nobody fights. Each guild gets its own pachinko board. Every picked chatter drops from the top, one ball at a time with the boards taking turns, bounces off the pins and lands in a slot worth points. The edge slots are rare and worth the most. A list on each board shows every player's own points as the balls land. The guild with the highest total wins, with marquee lights, a crown and a shower of confetti. A guild with fewer players gets the streamer's imps for the extra balls, so every guild drops as many. Like everything in the Arena, it is just for fun.

Emotes from everywhere

Fighters look like the last emote their chatter used. The game knows emotes from:

  • Twitch
  • BetterTTV
  • FrankerFaceZ
  • 7TV

Global sets and the channel's own sets are loaded without a login. Animated emotes move. Each source can be switched off. Chatters who never used an emote fight as a random global one, the same one every time.

Custom themes

A theme is a folder in overlay/themes/ with a theme.json. It can be as small as one replaced picture: a class badge, the archer's arrow, or a Monument stage. Bigger themes can set a font, colours, pixel scale, comic ink and halftone, or replace drawing functions with a script.

The map and statistics panels can be dragged to any spot in a preview in the control panel, or hidden.

What to expect

  • 100% free. No price, no subscription, no premium tier. Free as in lurking.
  • Runs on the streamer's own computer. No account, no central server and no bot in chat: the game reads chat, and writes in it only if the streamer switches on result announcements.
  • One download, nothing to install. Start it, and a setup guide in the browser walks through the channel name, the OBS browser source, the streamer's picture for the god and a first test battle.

Good to know

  • The game scales to your chat: 5 chatters and 5,000 feel the same.
  • You, the streamer, and known bots earn nothing. You can shut anyone out of the game.
  • Subs, bits and VIPs give nothing. No pay-to-win.
  • Each channel has its own save file. Progress carries over between streams.
  • The control panel tells you when a new version is out.
  • Keep Browser Source Hardware Acceleration on in OBS (Settings, Advanced). Without it the overlay draws on the processor and battles stutter; the control panel warns when it is off.

For viewers

Do I need to type anything?

No. Just chat like always. One message every few minutes is enough to count. More messages do not help.

How do I pick my guild?

Your Twitch name colour picks it the first time the game sees you this season. After that it is locked until the next season.

How do I get into a Clash or free-for-all?

Write any message while the countdown is on screen. One message is enough. Fighters are then drawn by lot. If you wrote in the few minutes before the countdown, you can still be drawn for places nobody else took.

What does my fighter look like?

It is the last emote you used, on a disc with a ring in your guild's colour and your name above it. The little badge shows your class for that fight.

Can I control my fighter?

No. All fights are automatic. Your part is showing up in chat.

Does spamming help my guild?

No. You earn at most one tick per time window of a few minutes, however many messages you write. Fifty messages count the same as one, so there is nothing to gain from flooding chat. Coming back stream after stream is what helps.

What actually happened

Chroma Clash is a fun side project by a computer scientist who has a day job and should probably have been sleeping. The computer scientist had ideas. The AI had everything else: every line of code, every hexagon, every pinball bumper, every rolling barrel, and the decision at three in the morning that a fantasy war game obviously needs Pachinko.

The human's job was to look at the result and say things like "the chatters look like edgy clouds" or "there are stutters every second". The AI then fixed them, said thank you, and kept going. This disclaimer was also written by the AI, about itself, in the third person, which is a little unsettling for everyone.

Lines of code written by the human: 0. Lines of code the human scrolled past while nodding wisely: all of them.

Frequently feared questions

  • Were any chatters harmed? No real ones. Several thousand simulated chatters were struck by lightning, set on fire and flung into bumpers, repeatedly, for testing.
  • Is the game balanced? Balancing is scheduled for "one pass at the end". The end has been rescheduled several times.
  • I found a bug. That is a feature the AI invented. If you found a feature, the human would like the credit.
  • Will the AI take over my stream? It cannot even type in your chat unless you hand it a token. It only wants to throw barrels at your viewers.
  • Was anything made by humans? Yes: the two fonts (Jersey 10 and Pirata One, by real type designers), Twitch, the emotes your chat posts, and the general concept of fun.
